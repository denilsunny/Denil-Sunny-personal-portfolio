Flight Controller & IMU

The quadcopter was built around an ESP32-S3 Mini as the main flight-control microcontroller and an MPU6050 6-DoF IMU for real-time acceleration and angular-velocity measurements. The MPU6050 communicates with the ESP32-S3 through the I²C interface, providing the motion data required for flight stabilization.

Motor Drive System

Instead of using a dedicated motor-driver module, I implemented four individual low-side MOSFET motor-driver stages, one for each motor. Each stage uses an AO3400 N-channel MOSFET, an SS14 Schottky diode, and a 10 kΩ gate resistor to control an 8520 coreless motor.

The ESP32-S3 generates independent PWM control signals for the four motor channels. Each PWM signal is applied to the corresponding MOSFET gate, allowing the controller to regulate the motor drive. The MOSFET switches the motor current on the low side, while the Schottky diode provides a flyback path for the inductive motor during switching. The 10 kΩ resistor provides a defined gate state when the PWM signal is inactive.

AO3400 — N-Channel MOSFET
Acts as the low-side switch controlling each motor independently. Driven by PWM gate signals from the ESP32-S3 to vary motor speed with high efficiency.
×4 units
SS14 — Schottky Flyback Diode
Placed in parallel with each motor to clamp inductive voltage spikes generated during PWM switching, protecting the MOSFET from back-EMF damage.
×4 units
10 kΩ Gate Resistor
Pulls the MOSFET gate to a known low state when no PWM signal is applied, ensuring the switch is fully off at idle and preventing spurious turn-on.

8520 Coreless Motor
High-speed coreless DC motors selected for their lightweight form factor and rapid response characteristics — ideal for the weight constraints of this micro-quadcopter build.

Power Supply

The complete system is powered by a Pro-Range 3.7 V 1000 mAh 30C 1S Lithium Polymer (Li-Po) battery, selected for its lightweight form factor and high current-delivery capability required by the four coreless motors.

The battery provides a 3.7 V nominal voltage, 1000 mAh capacity, and a 30C discharge rating — corresponding to a theoretical maximum continuous discharge capability of approximately 30 A. Its high-current discharge leads help minimize resistance and voltage drop during high-load motor operation.

Control Interface & Wireless Communication

The quadcopter is controlled wirelessly using the open-source Espressif ESP-Drone mobile application. The ESP32-S3 establishes a Wi-Fi connection with the smartphone, providing the communication link between the mobile control interface and the flight-control system.

The control process involves connecting the smartphone to the drone’s Wi-Fi network, granting the required location permission, and establishing the connection through the application’s Connect interface. Once connected, the application provides virtual controls for the primary flight parameters: Thrust, Roll, Pitch, and Yaw.

Control commands are transmitted wirelessly to the ESP32-S3, where they are processed by the flight-control firmware and translated into appropriate PWM outputs for the four motor drivers.

Mechanical Integration

The complete hardware was integrated into a 3D-printed quadcopter frame based on an open-source design. The frame provides the structural platform for the four motors, flight controller, IMU, motor-driver circuitry, and battery.

The resulting platform combines 3D-printed mechanical fabrication, discrete motor-control electronics, inertial sensing, wireless control, power delivery, and embedded flight-control processing into a compact quadcopter system.

This build intentionally spans three engineering disciplines in a single platform — embedded firmware, analog/power electronics, and mechanical design — demonstrating end-to-end system integration from component selection through to a flying result.
