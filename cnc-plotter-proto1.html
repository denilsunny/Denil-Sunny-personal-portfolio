Project Overview

Designed and developed a compact CNC plotter prototype by repurposing the mechanical assemblies of a discarded DVD player. The system uses an ESP32 microcontroller for wireless G-code reception and motion control, enabling digital vector drawings to be converted into physical plots on paper.

By salvaging the precision linear rails, stepper lead screws, and optical laser pick-up carriages from optical drives, this prototype achieves micron-grade mechanical guidance at virtually zero fabrication cost. The ESP32 hosts a local Wi-Fi interface, parsing incoming machine coordinates in real time and synchronizing dual stepper motors and a pen-lifting servo to reproduce complex 2D vector art with high repeatability.

Hardware Architecture

The system integrates an ESP32 as the primary Wi-Fi-enabled controller, dual 28BYJ-48 stepper motors driven through ULN2003 Darlington arrays for planar movement, an SG90 micro servo for pen up/down actuation, a dedicated buck converter for clean power distribution, and repurposed DVD player drive mechanisms for linear translation.

ESP32 — Wi-Fi-Enabled Main Controller
Acts as the central processing unit and wireless server. Configured as a local Wi-Fi access point / receiver to accept G-code toolpaths transmitted over the air, parse Cartesian coordinates in real time, generate step pulse trains, and control the pen-lift servo.
Microcontroller & Wi-Fi Hub
2 × 28BYJ-48 Stepper Motors — X/Y Axis Movement
Provide high-torque, precise rotational steps to drive the leadscrews of the X and Y axes. Their internal reduction gearhead ensures dependable holding torque and accurate incremental translation without position drift.
Dual-Axis Actuation
2 × ULN2003 — Stepper Motor Drivers
Darlington transistor array driver boards that amplify the low-current logic signals from the ESP32 GPIOs into high-current pulses to energize the four coils of each 28BYJ-48 unipolar stepper motor in correct sequence.
Motor Drivers
SG90 Servo — Pen Lifting Mechanism (Z-Axis)
Lightweight 9g micro servo mapped to the Z-axis commands. Controls the mechanical arm that lifts the pen during rapid transit (Pen-Up / G00) and gently presses it onto the paper during drawing strokes (Pen-Down / G01).
Z-Axis Actuator
Buck Converter — Regulated Power Supply
High-efficiency DC-DC step-down buck converter providing a rock-solid, regulated 5V power bus. Delivers the continuous current demand of both stepper motors, drivers, and the SG90 servo without voltage sag or reset triggers on the ESP32.
Power Regulation
DVD Player Mechanisms — Repurposed Linear-Motion System
Salvaged laser sled assemblies from optical drives featuring hardened ground guide rods and precision lead screws. Serves as a ready-made, backlash-minimal linear slide system offering exceptional smoothness and rigidity in an ultra-compact footprint.
Mechanical Assemblies

01
Vector Drawing Preparation (Inkscape)
The user creates or imports 2D vector artwork in Inkscape. Graphic objects, text, and outlines are scaled to fit within the plotter’s active drawing area (roughly 40×40 mm, defined by the DVD carriage travel) and converted into continuous stroke paths.
02
CAM Toolpath & G-Code Conversion (JScut)
The vector SVG is processed through JScut, an open-source CAM tool, to generate machine-readable G-code. Standard rapid transit (G00), linear interpolation (G01), and Z-axis lift/lower triggers are computed with appropriate feedrates and step resolutions.
03
Wireless G-Code Streaming over Local Wi-Fi
The ESP32 is configured as a local Wi-Fi-enabled controller, broadcasting its own wireless network or connecting to a local LAN. A laptop or local client device connects wirelessly to stream the generated G-code file directly to the ESP32 without requiring tethered USB cables.
04
Real-Time Coordinate Parsing on ESP32
The firmware running on the ESP32 interprets incoming G-code lines on the fly. It calculates the necessary step counts and directional signals for the X and Y axes using Bresenham-style line interpolation, maintaining precise synchronization across both planar axes.
05
Stepper Actuation via ULN2003 Drivers
The motion commands drive the two 28BYJ-48 stepper motors through their dedicated ULN2003 driver boards. The motors rotate the DVD mechanism lead screws, translating rotational steps into smooth, high-precision linear travel along the horizontal and vertical beds.
06
Servo-Controlled Pen Up / Down & Plotting
The SG90 micro servo receives PWM angles to instantly raise or lower the pen holder. When moving between disconnected lines, the pen is lifted; during draw commands, the pen is gently lowered against the paper, accurately transferring the digital drawing into a tangible physical plot.

System Workflow Summary:
Inkscape → JScut → G-code → Wi-Fi → ESP32 → Stepper Drivers → X/Y Motion + Servo → Physical Plot
